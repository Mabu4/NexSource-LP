In short: Supplier evaluation is the regular, criteria-based assessment of your suppliers. ISO 9001 requires it in clause 8.4. It only becomes genuinely useful when criteria are weighted, kept constant over years, and tied to concrete actions.

Five common mistakes

  1. Once a year, only for the audit. Scores produced from memory in December are worthless.
  2. Equal weighting everywhere. A good price then compensates for terrible delivery reliability — usually the wrong trade.
  3. One person scoring alone. Quality belongs to QA, delivery to planning, terms to procurement.
  4. No feedback to the supplier. A supplier who never sees their score cannot improve. The feedback conversation is the actual lever.
  5. No history. Without a year-on-year comparison you see level but not trend — and trend is the more interesting signal.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which criteria belong in a supplier evaluation?

A mix of hard and soft criteria works best: delivery reliability, quality and price as the core, plus communication, flexibility, problem solving and evidence. What matters is less the exact selection than consistency — trends only become visible if you score the same way year after year.

How often should suppliers be evaluated?

A-suppliers at least annually, ideally twice a year. B and C suppliers annually or after an incident. Evaluating more often rarely produces new insight; less often makes the evaluation worthless.

Is supplier evaluation required by ISO 9001?

ISO 9001:2015 clause 8.4 requires you to determine and apply criteria for the evaluation, selection, monitoring of performance and re-evaluation of external providers, and to retain documented information on the results and any necessary actions. The method is up to you; the evidence is not.
